Joining bonus: tax, clawback and what you actually keep

A joining bonus is a one-time payment for accepting an offer. It is taxed like salary, it is often in the CTC for the first year only, and you may have to pay it back if you leave early. Here is what it is worth after tax.

The short version

  • If you leave within the clawback period (often 12 months), you usually repay the full amount before tax, not what you received.

How clawbacks work

Most joining bonuses come with a clause: if you leave within a set period, often 12 months, you pay it back. Read it closely before you sign:

  • Gross or net? Most clauses ask for the full amount before tax. On ₹20 LPA, you received about {{JB_20_2_NET}} of a ₹2 lakh bonus, but you would repay ₹2,00,000.
  • All or part? Some companies reduce the amount for each month you stayed. Ask for this if it isn’t offered.
  • Getting the tax back is not always simple. If the repayment happens in the same financial year, your employer can often correct it in payroll. Across years, it is harder: ask a tax professional.

Before you accept

  • Ask whether the joining bonus is included in the CTC. If it is, your second year’s pay will be lower than the offer suggests.
  • Ask when it is paid: with your first salary, or after 3 or 6 months.
  • Compare offers on fixed pay. A joining bonus is paid once, while a higher fixed salary pays every year and raises every future hike. See how to read your offer letter.

Frequently asked questions

Is a joining bonus taxable?

Yes. It is part of your salary for the year it is paid, and your employer deducts tax on it.

Does my salary from my old job matter?

Yes. Tax is worked out on your total income for the financial year, including salary from your previous employer. Tell your new employer about it, or you may owe more tax when you file your return.

Is a joining bonus the same as a retention bonus?

No. A joining bonus is paid for accepting the offer. A retention bonus is paid later, for staying a set time.

Can I negotiate a joining bonus?

Often, yes, especially to make up for a bonus or variable pay you lose by leaving your current job. Ask for the clawback terms in writing.
