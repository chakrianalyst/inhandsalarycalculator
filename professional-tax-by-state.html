Professional tax: what you actually pay

Professional tax is a small tax that some Indian states charge on salaried people. Your employer deducts it from your pay every month and pays it to the state, so it is one of the things that make your in-hand salary a little lower than your gross pay.

How much is it?

  • The law limits it to ₹2,500 a year for any one person.
  • In most states that charge it, the monthly amount is at most about ₹200 to ₹210, and people on low pay pay less or nothing.
  • Some states, such as Karnataka and Maharashtra, charge a little more in February so that the year adds up to ₹2,500.

On a ₹12 lakh salary, ₹2,400 to ₹2,500 a year is about 0.2% of your pay. It matters for getting your payslip number exactly right, but it will not change a decision.

Which states charge it?

Many states do, including Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Madhya Pradesh. Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan do not charge it on salaries.

Each state sets its own pay slabs, and some treat men and women differently or work out the amount on half-yearly pay. States also revise their slabs from time to time. For example, West Bengal introduced a new schedule from 1 October 2026.

Why our calculator asks you for the amount

Because the rules differ by state and change often, a calculator that guessed would sometimes be wrong. Your payslip shows the exact figure, so we ask you to enter it. The default is ₹200 a month, the most common amount. Enter 0 if your state does not charge it.

Does it reduce my income tax?

Under the old tax regime, professional tax is deducted from your salary before tax is worked out. Under the new regime it is not. Either way, it is taken out of your take-home pay.

Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum professional tax?

The law caps it at ₹2,500 a year per person.

Where do I find my professional tax amount?

On your payslip, usually under deductions as “PT” or “professional tax”.

Is professional tax part of income tax?

No. It is a separate state tax. Under the old regime it can be deducted from taxable salary.
