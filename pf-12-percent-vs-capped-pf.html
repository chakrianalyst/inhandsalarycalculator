12% PF or capped ₹3,000 PF: which gives you more in hand?

Many offers let you choose between PF at 12% of your basic salary and PF capped at ₹3,000 a month (it was ₹1,800 until September 2026). One puts more in your bank every month, the other puts more into your PF. Here is the difference at every CTC.

What the two options mean

PF is 12% of your basic salary from you, and the same again from your employer. The law only requires it on pay up to the PF wage ceiling of ₹25,000 a month, which is ₹3,000 from each side. So when your basic is above ₹25,000, many companies let you choose: 12% of your full basic, or PF capped at ₹3,000 a month.

The ceiling was ₹15,000 a month, giving the well-known fixed ₹1,800 PF, until 16 September 2026. It rose to ₹25,000 on 17 September 2026, so the old ₹1,800 cap is now ₹3,000. If your payslip still shows ₹1,800, ask HR when it will change.

Some employers may calculate PF on the labour codes’ wider “wages” base (at least half of your total pay) instead of basic. That raises both options when your basic is under half of your pay. Ask HR which base your company uses.

In most offers with this choice, your CTC stays the same either way. When the employer’s PF drops to ₹3,000, the difference is paid to you as salary instead. That is why capped PF raises your in-hand twice: less is taken from your pay, and more is added to it. The extra salary is taxed, so a little of it goes in tax.

How to choose

  • Capped ₹3,000 PF suits you if you would invest the extra yourself, need the cash now (rent, a loan EMI, saving for a deposit), or already save a lot elsewhere.
  • 12% PF suits you if you know you would spend the extra. PF is savings you cannot easily touch, earning a steady rate with no tax on the interest within the limits.
  • In the old tax regime, your own PF counts towards the ₹1.5 lakh section 80C limit. With capped PF, you would need other 80C investments to use the full limit. The new regime gives no deduction for PF either way.

Frequently asked questions

Does capped PF change my CTC?

Usually not. Your CTC stays the same, and the employer PF you no longer receive is paid to you as salary. Check your offer letter: a few companies keep their own share at 12% and change only yours, which gives a smaller increase in hand.

Does capped PF reduce my gratuity?

No. Gratuity is worked out from your basic salary (or wages), not from your PF, so it stays the same.

What happened to the fixed ₹1,800 PF?

₹1,800 was 12% of the old ₹15,000 wage ceiling. The ceiling rose to ₹25,000 a month on 17 September 2026, so the smallest PF for anyone with a basic of ₹25,000 or more is now ₹3,000 a month from you and ₹3,000 from your employer.

Can I change my choice later?

It depends on your employer. Many fix it at joining or allow a change once a year. Ask HR before you sign.
